The method

Three guarantees, and how each one is kept.

Represent makes exactly three promises. Everything below is how each is enforced in practice — including where the limits are.

01 — Identity

Every voter is a verified human.

Before you can vote, you verify once: a government-issued ID and a liveness check — a short selfie capture that defeats a photo of a photo — handled by a certified identity provider.

Your region comes from the document itself, not a dropdown you fill in, which is what makes a local ballot actually local.

Represent never stores your documents. The images stay inside the provider's infrastructure. What we keep is the decision — verified or not, and the region — attached to a one-way hash. Our database holds no passport images to leak, because we never receive them.

Verification is free to you. We treat the cost as ours to carry.

02 — The threshold

No result until ten people have voted.

A public ballot shows no split until ten verified people have voted on it. Until then you see how many ballots exist and your own receipt — nothing else.

This is deliberate, and it costs us engagement. A running tally changes how people vote: they join the side that's winning. Hiding it until there are enough ballots to mean something removes the bandwagon, and removes the ability to quote a landslide that was really three votes.

When a ballot closes below the threshold, the count is recorded but the split is never shown. A number too small to mean anything doesn't get published just because it exists.

Below the threshold, you see

6 of 10 verified ballots

Your own ballot: recorded and counted. Everyone else's: not yet.

03 — The record

Checkable by someone who doesn't trust us.

Every ballot is written twice: to our database, so the app is instant, and to a public blockchain ledger, so the count can be checked by someone who doesn't trust us.

That second write is the point. Any platform can show you a number. Far fewer let you verify that number independently — and a company that can silently edit its own results is asking you to take its word.

The ledger records that a ballot was cast and sealed. It does not record who you are.

Where this stands today

The public ledger is running in pilot phase on a test network while the system is proven. The method, the contract addresses and a worked example are published on the methodology page — including what is not yet finished.

What Represent does not do

It makes a count undeniable. That is all it makes.

It doesn't bind anyone

A Represent ballot is not a legal vote and does not obligate any government or institution to act. It produces evidence, not authority.

It doesn't prove a whole population

A result reflects the verified people who voted — not a random sample of everyone. It is a census of participants, not a poll.

It doesn't take a side

Represent has no position on any question asked on it. The only interest is that the count is real.
