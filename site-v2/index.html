The problem

386,698

signatures on the largest petition in Canadian history — dismissed in a single news cycle.

Not because the cause was wrong.

Because nobody could prove the signers were real people. That is the flaw in every online poll, petition and consultation: a name costs nothing to invent, so a number built from names proves nothing.

Generating thousands of convincing accounts is now trivial. Every unverified number online is suspect by default — and the people who actually signed get thrown out with the fakes.

The rule

No result until enough real people have voted.

A ballot stays sealed until ten verified people have voted on it. Nobody sees a running tally, so nobody votes with the crowd — and nobody can spin a result out of the first three votes.

How it works

Three things, and no more.

01

Verified identity

A government ID and a liveness check, through a certified provider, once. Your location comes from the document itself — not a dropdown. Represent never stores your documents.

02

One person, one vote

Duplicate accounts can't vote twice, because the same person can't verify twice. The count is a count of people, not of clicks.

03

A public record

Every ballot is written to a public ledger as well as our database, so the count can be audited independently rather than taken on trust. The ledger is running in pilot phase.
